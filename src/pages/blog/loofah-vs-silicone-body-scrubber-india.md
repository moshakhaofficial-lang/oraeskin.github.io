Most Indian bathrooms have a loofah hanging in them. Silicone body scrubbers are now climbing Amazon India's bestseller list too. Is it worth switching?

**The hygiene problem with loofahs:** mesh and natural loofahs stay damp in warm, humid bathrooms and trap dead skin. That makes them a good home for bacteria. If you shave your legs or have small cuts or body acne, that bacteria has an easy way in.

**Why silicone is different:** it doesn't absorb water, rinses clean and dries quickly, so far fewer bacteria build up.

**Either way, don't over-scrub.** Hard physical scrubbing can irritate skin and darken it on elbows, knees and the back. For bumpy skin, body acne or tan, gentle **chemical exfoliants** in body washes and lotions usually work better.

## How to Exfoliate Your Body Safely

1. **Scrub gently, two or three times a week**, not daily. Light pressure is enough.
2. **Hang your scrubber to dry outside the shower** if you can, and wash silicone brushes with soap every week.
3. **Replace mesh loofahs every 3–4 weeks**, or sooner if they smell or change colour.
4. **Don't scrub freshly shaved or waxed skin**, sunburn or active body acne.
5. **For bumps, back acne or tan, use chemical exfoliants instead:**

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Are loofahs unhygienic?
They can be. Damp loofahs trap dead skin and grow bacteria, especially in humid Indian bathrooms. Hang them to dry and replace them every 3–4 weeks. Silicone scrubbers are more hygienic because they dry quickly and rinse clean.

### Is a silicone scrubber good for back acne?
It is fine for gentle cleansing, but don't scrub hard over active spots. A salicylic acid body wash such as [Minimalist 2% Salicylic Acid Body Wash](/reviews/minimalist-salicylic-bodywash/) does more for back acne.
