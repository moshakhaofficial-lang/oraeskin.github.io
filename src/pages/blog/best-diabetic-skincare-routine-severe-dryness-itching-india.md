## How Diabetes Damages the Cutaneous Barrier

Elevated systemic glucose levels cause profound biochemical changes throughout all layers of the skin:

1. **Osmotic Diuresis & Intracellular Dehydration**: Chronic hyperglycemia prompts the kidneys to excrete excess glucose in urine, pulling large volumes of water with it. This systemic fluid depletion robs the stratum corneum of essential moisture, leading to parched, parchment-like skin.
2. **Advanced Glycation End-Products (AGEs)**: Excess circulating glucose molecules cross-link non-enzymatically with dermal collagen and elastin fibers. This stiffens connective tissues, impairs skin elasticity, and drastically slows down cellular renewal.
3. **Cutaneous Microangiopathy**: Diabetic small-vessel disease reduces microvascular blood flow to the skin, starving epidermal cells of oxygen and nutrients. Sebaceous and sweat gland function declines (autonomic neuropathy), severely impairing natural skin lubrication.
4. **Impaired Antimicrobial Defense & Pruritus**: Elevated sugar levels in sweat and skin interstitial fluid feed pathogenic fungi (*Candida*, *Trichophyton*) and bacteria (*Staphylococcus*), triggering severe itching, micro-cracks, and slow-healing secondary infections.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Why does diabetes make feet and shins so terribly itchy?

Autonomic neuropathy in diabetes impairs sweat and oil gland function in the lower extremities, while peripheral artery disease reduces blood supply. This causes severe local xerosis and neuropathic itch sensations that are worse at night.

### Q: Can diabetics use chemical exfoliants or foot peels?

Never use harsh chemical foot peels (such as high-percentage peeling booties) or physical pumice stones without consulting a physician. Diabetics have delayed wound healing and diminished pain sensation; small cuts can rapidly evolve into serious diabetic foot ulcers.

### Q: Why is moisturizing within 3 minutes of bathing so important?

Bathing softens the stratum corneum and temporarily hydrates it. However, within minutes, that water evaporates into dry ambient air, siphoning internal skin water with it. Applying a ceramide cream within 3 minutes locks that water inside.
