High-concentration AHA/BHA chemical peeling solutions can cause severe burns on melanin-rich skin. Learn the clinical safety rules for weekly exfoliation.

Viral social media trends often encourage beginners to slather 25%-30% AHA chemical peeling solutions across their faces. In melanin-rich Indian skin (Fitzpatrick skin types III-VI), high acid concentrations cause rapid protein coagulation, chemical acid burns, and severe post-inflammatory hyperpigmentation. Weekly chemical exfoliation must be introduced using large-molecule PHAs or buffered AHAs with rigorous safety timers.

## Editorial Routine Tips for Indian Skin

1. **The 10-Minute Maximum Rule**: If using concentrated acid solutions, never leave them on for longer than 8 to 10 minutes. Wash off immediately with cool water.
2. **Never Apply on Wet Skin**: Applying concentrated chemical acids to damp skin increases acid penetration velocity by 3x, dramatically raising chemical burn risks. Skin must be bone-dry.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why did my chemical peel turn my skin darker?
A concentrated acid burn triggers acute inflammation. Melanocytes in Indian skin rush to produce pigment as a wound-healing response, creating dark post-peel patches (PIH).

### How often should I use a chemical peel at home?
Never more than once a week! For sensitive skin, start once every two weeks using gentle PHAs.
