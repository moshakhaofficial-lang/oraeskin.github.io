In reality, a dark neck is almost always caused by either Acanthosis Nigricans (insulin-resistance induced hyperkeratosis) or chronic UV sun tanning.

## When to See a Doctor

Velvety, thickened dark skin on the neck, armpits or groin (acanthosis nigricans) is often a sign of **insulin resistance**, and can appear with PCOS, prediabetes or type 2 diabetes. It is worth asking your doctor for a **blood sugar check (such as HbA1c)**, especially if it appeared recently, is spreading, or you have a family history of diabetes. Treating the underlying cause is what makes the biggest difference; skincare can only improve the surface. See a doctor promptly if dark patches appear suddenly or spread quickly.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### What is Acanthosis Nigricans?
It is a metabolic skin condition characterized by dark, velvety, papillomatous thickening of skin in body folds (neck, armpits, knuckles), commonly associated with high circulating insulin or PCOS.

### Can skincare alone cure Acanthosis Nigricans?
Topical exfoliants like Glycolic acid significantly lighten surface velvety thickness, but lifestyle management (dietary changes, lowering insulin resistance) is essential for complete resolution.
