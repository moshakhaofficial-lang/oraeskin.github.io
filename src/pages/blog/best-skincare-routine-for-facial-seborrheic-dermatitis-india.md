Struggling with itchy, red, greasy flakes around your nose folds, eyebrows, and ears? Discover a Malassezia-safe dermatological routine for facial seborrheic dermatitis.

Facial seborrheic dermatitis is a chronic inflammatory dermatosis caused by an abnormal immune reaction to Malassezia yeast proliferating in sebaceous-rich areas (nasolabial folds, eyebrow margins, glabella, and behind the ears). It manifests as red, greasy, yellowish scales that sting when washed. Applying traditional heavy moisturizers containing plant oils or fatty esters feeds the yeast and worsens flaking.

## When to See a Doctor

Seborrhoeic dermatitis is linked to *Malassezia* yeast, so gentle skincare alone often isn't enough. A doctor or dermatologist may suggest an **antifungal** such as a ketoconazole cream or wash, and sometimes a short course of a mild anti-inflammatory cream. See one if flaking and redness don't improve after 2–3 weeks of gentle care, if the rash spreads, or if it is very itchy or painful. Avoid using steroid creams from a pharmacy without advice, as they can make the condition rebound.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Why is seborrheic dermatitis both oily and flaky?
The flakes are not caused by dry skin; they are inflammatory dead skin cells bound together by oxidized sebum produced in response to Malassezia yeast activity.

### Does seborrheic dermatitis flare with stress?
Yes, physical and emotional stress upregulates cortisol, which increases sebaceous gland lipid secretion and impairs barrier immunity.
