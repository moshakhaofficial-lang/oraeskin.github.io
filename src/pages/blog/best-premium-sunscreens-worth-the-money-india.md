Dermatologists in India often recommend imported or "derm-brand" sunscreens such as La Roche-Posay, Heliocare, Cetaphil and UV Doux. They cost two to five times more than popular Indian brands. Are they worth it?

**The honest answer:** the best sunscreen is the one you will apply generously every morning and reapply when you are outdoors. A face needs about a quarter-teaspoon, roughly two finger-lengths. If a ₹2,000 tube makes you skimp, a ₹450 tube used properly protects you better.

**When paying more can make sense:**
- **Melasma or stubborn pigmentation.** Some premium formulas protect further into long UVA and visible light, which drive this kind of pigment.
- **Very oily or acne-prone skin** that hasn't got on with cheaper textures.
- **Sensitive skin** that needs a fragrance-free formula with a proven filter system.

We compared four premium sunscreens on Amazon India with a ₹448 alternative and scored each with our [formulation-based method](/how-we-review/).

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is an expensive sunscreen more protective than a cheap one?
Not automatically. SPF and PA ratings measure protection, and a well-made ₹450 SPF 50 PA++++ sunscreen protects very well. Premium formulas mainly add a nicer texture, newer filters for long UVA, or antioxidants. These can matter for melasma, but they matter less than applying enough.

### Which is the best value?
[Minimalist Light Fluid SPF 50](/reviews/minimalist-light-fluid-spf50/) at about ₹9 per ml. Of the premium four, [UV Doux](/reviews/uv-doux-sunscreen-gel/) is cheapest at about ₹15.5 per gram. Prices were checked on Amazon.in on 22 November 2026 and change often.

### Which are fragrance-free?
[Cetaphil Sun](/reviews/cetaphil-sun-spf50-gel/), [UV Doux](/reviews/uv-doux-sunscreen-gel/) and [Minimalist Light Fluid](/reviews/minimalist-light-fluid-spf50/) are unscented. Heliocare's listing gives a citrus scent. La Roche-Posay's Amazon listing doesn't print its ingredients.

### How much sunscreen should I use?
About a quarter-teaspoon for the face and neck, or two finger-lengths. Most people use half that, which gives much less protection than the label says.
