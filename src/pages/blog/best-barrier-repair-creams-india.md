## Is Your Skin Barrier Damaged?

**Common signs:**
- Stinging or burning when you apply products that never bothered you before.
- Tightness after washing, even with a gentle cleanser.
- Redness, rough patches or flaking.
- Sudden breakouts or small bumps along with sensitivity.

**Common causes in India:** over-exfoliating with acids or scrubs, starting a retinoid too fast, harsh face washes, salon bleach or facials, very hot water, and dry winter air or air conditioning.

## How to Repair Your Skin Barrier

1. **Pause all actives for 1–2 weeks:** acids, retinoids, vitamin C, scrubs and peel pads.
2. **Wash with a gentle, fragrance-free cleanser** and lukewarm water, once or twice a day. See our [cleansers for sensitive skin](/blog/best-cleansers-for-sensitive-red-skin-india/).
3. **Apply a barrier cream twice a day**, ideally on slightly damp skin.
4. **Keep using sunscreen**, preferably a gentle or [mineral sunscreen](/blog/best-mineral-physical-sunscreens-india/).
5. **Bring actives back slowly**, one at a time, two or three nights a week.

Mild damage usually settles in 1–2 weeks; more severe irritation can take a month. **See a dermatologist** if skin is raw, weeping, very swollen, or not improving after two weeks, or if the damage came from steroid creams. ### How long does it take to repair a damaged skin barrier?
Mild damage usually improves in 1–2 weeks once you stop actives and use a barrier cream twice a day. More severe irritation can take 4–6 weeks.

### Are ceramide creams good for oily skin?
Yes, if the texture is light. Ceramides don't clog pores by themselves, but rich creams can feel heavy. Oily skin often does better with a gel such as [Minimalist B5](/reviews/minimalist-b5-moisturizer/), or a thin layer of Dot & Key at night.
