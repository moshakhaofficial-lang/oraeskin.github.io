## The Cause of Post-Threading Bumps & Irritation

The skin along the superciliary arch (eyebrow ridge) is thin, highly vascularized, and rich in delicate sensory nerve endings. Threading disrupts this zone through three primary mechanisms:

1. **Mechanical Follicular Avulsion & Edema**: Cotton thread grips and forcefully pulls hair follicles from deep within the dermis. This produces acute micro-trauma, causing the follicle wall to swell shut and triggering localized histamine release (producing immediate hives and redness).
2. **Bacterial Inoculation from Cotton Thread & Hands**: Salon threading involves holding one end of the cotton thread in the aesthetician's mouth while tensioning it across your skin. If the aesthetician does not sanitize hands or uses unsterilized thread, oral and skin bacteria (*Staphylococcus epidermidis* and *S. aureus*) are directly seeded into open, traumatized follicles.
3. **The 'Salon Astringent' Chemical Burn**: Many parlors aggressively slap on cheap, high-alcohol astringents or cooling gels loaded with artificial menthol and green dyes immediately after threading. On freshly lacerated skin, these harsh chemicals cause acute contact dermatitis, burning, and subsequent flaky peeling.

## The Foolproof Post-Threading Care Protocol

### 1. Before Threading (At Home or Salon)
- **Wash the Brow Ridge**: Cleanse your face with [Simple Refreshing Facial Wash](https://www.amazon.in/dp/B0FJMB2NZM?tag=oraeskin-21) before your appointment to remove oil and grime. Never thread over foundation or makeup.
- **Instruct Your Aesthetician**: Politely request fresh, unused cotton thread and ask them to sanitize their hands with alcohol hand rub before touching your face.

### 2. Immediately After Threading (At the Salon)
- **Say NO to Cheap Salon Lotions**: Decline the generic parlor 'soothing cream' or artificial green astringent.
- **Apply Ice or Cool Water**: Press a clean tissue wrapped around an ice cube against the brow ridge for 60 seconds to constrict capillaries and suppress histamine swelling.

- **No Heavy Makeup or Active Acids**: Avoid brow pencils, concealers, AHAs, BHAs, and retinol near the brow arch for at least 24 to 48 hours.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Why do I get tiny whiteheads 2 days after threading?

When hair is pulled out, the follicle is wide open. If bacteria from fingers, salon thread, or comedogenic creams enter the follicle, your body sends white blood cells to combat the infection, forming tiny white pus-filled pimples (folliculitis). Keeping the area clean and sealed with a sterile barrier balm prevents this entirely.

### Q: Is eyebrow waxing better than threading for sensitive skin?

Waxing removes hair faster over larger areas, but it also strips the topmost epidermal layer of skin, carrying a higher risk of burns and skin tearing around the thin eye contour. Threading is generally safer for skin elasticity when performed cleanly.

### Q: Can I apply aloe vera gel from my garden plant?

Fresh raw aloe vera contains aloin and latex compounds just beneath the rind, which are potent irritants that frequently cause contact dermatitis on broken, freshly threaded skin. Stick to dermatological sterile balms instead.
