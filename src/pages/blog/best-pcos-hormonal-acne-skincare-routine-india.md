## Why PCOS Causes Deep Jawline & Chin Breakouts in Indian Skin

Polycystic Ovary Syndrome (PCOS) involves hyperandrogenism—an excess of circulating androgens (such as testosterone and DHEAS) or increased cellular sensitivity to normal androgen levels. In sebaceous glands, the enzyme 5-alpha reductase converts testosterone into dihydrotestosterone (DHT), which docks onto androgen receptors clustered densely along the jawline and chin.

This triggers two major pathophysiological events:
1. **Sebaceous Gland Hyperplasia & Hypersecretion**: Glands produce thick, viscous sebum rich in squalene and wax esters.
2. **Follicular Hyperkeratinization**: Dead corneocytes lining the hair follicle fail to shed naturally, creating a sticky keratin plug (microcomedone).

When *Cutibacterium acnes* bacteria thrive in this oxygen-deprived sebum plug, your immune system launches a deep neutrophil reaction, resulting in painful, red, non-draining cystic nodules. Because Indian skin (Fitzpatrick skin types III to V) has active, labile melanocytes, squeezing or harshly treating these cysts almost inevitably leaves stubborn Post-Inflammatory Hyperpigmentation (PIH) that can linger for 6 to 12 months.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Q: Can topical skincare alone cure PCOS acne?

Topical skincare effectively manages surface follicular blockages, bacterial proliferation, and hyperpigmentation. However, because PCOS acne originates from endocrine imbalances (elevated androgens and insulin resistance), combining topical dermatological care with lifestyle interventions and medical advice from a gynecologist or endocrinologist yields the most sustainable, long-term remission.

### Q: Why does my hormonal acne flare up right before my menstrual cycle?

During the luteal phase (the 7 to 10 days preceding menstruation), estrogen levels drop while progesterone peaks, leaving relative androgen dominance. This causes sebaceous glands to produce thicker sebum and narrows the pore lumen, triggering pre-menstrual acne flare-ups.

### Q: Should I squeeze or pop deep hormonal cysts?

Never. Hormonal cysts are located deep within the dermis and do not have an open pore head. Squeezing drives bacterial toxins deeper into surrounding tissue, causing follicular rupture, severe cellulitis, and permanent atrophic scarring.
